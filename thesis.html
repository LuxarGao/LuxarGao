Master’s thesis · University of Chicago · 2025

The Algorithm Will Not Babysit

Chinese women’s conception of fertility in the tension between birth-policy propaganda and fertility posts on Xiaohongshu and Douyin.

Full thesis · PDF ↓Institutional record ↗

Question

How is reproductive citizenship negotiated on platforms?

The study asks how national pronatalist policy shapes reproductive roles and how women use platformed feminist critique to resist, reinterpret, and reimagine those roles.

58,983collected comments

Methods

Computational patterns, lived experience.

Python-based cleaning and text analysis map large-scale discourse across Xiaohongshu and Douyin. Semi-structured interviews and MAXQDA thematic coding connect those patterns to women’s accounts of fertility, marriage, labor, bodily risk, and autonomy.

Findings

63%unwilling to have children
28%willing
9%neutral

Recurring concerns include pregnancy-related physical harm, marriage quality, economic pressure, unequal motherhood responsibility, and distrust of policy propaganda. Desire, family continuity, and affective motivations remain present but are negotiated against structural risks.
